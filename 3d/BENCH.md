# Servo characterisation stands

Three printed test stands that **measure** the ST3215, so leg motion can be found by
searching joint space instead of solved by inverse kinematics.

The model is generated by [servo_bench.py](servo_bench.py):

```bash
.venv/bin/python servo_bench.py            # → out/bench/{step,stl}, out/bench/bom.json
.venv/bin/python servo_bench.py --render   # … and out/bench/view_{a,b,c}_{iso,side}.png
```

Every servo dimension, fit, clearance, nut pocket, density and mass in it is imported from
`mini_dog.py`. Nothing is re-typed — see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), "Masses and densities live
once".

## Why they exist

`../ros2/smalldog_walker` plans with a closed-form leg IK: a foot target goes in, three
joint angles come out, and the servo is assumed to follow. Replacing that with an
enumeration — generate candidate joint trajectories, score them, keep the feasible ones —
changes what the model has to know. IK needs link lengths, and this repo measures those to
0.1 mm off the vendor STEP. A search needs the **actuator**, and every actuator number here
is a catalogue figure or a guess:

| number | today | where |
|---|---|---|
| `SERVO_STALL_NM` = 2.94 | "30 kg·cm at 12 V", vendor | `mini_dog.py` §4 |
| `SERVO_NOLOAD_RADS` = 4.71 | "0.222 s / 60° at 12 V", vendor, unloaded | `mini_dog.py` §4 |
| `SERVO_KG` = 0.060 | vendor, already marked **verify** | `mini_dog.py` §4 |
| `MJ_KP` / `MJ_DAMPING` / `MJ_ARMATURE` / `MJ_FRICTIONLOSS` | "Not measured — the ST3215 gearbox is a black box" | `export_sim.py` |

A search filters every candidate against exactly those numbers, so a search built on them is
a search over a robot that does not exist. Two more quantities it needs are not in the model
at all: the joint's **compliance** — the ±0.9°/joint in the thrust-clamp note is arithmetic
on the print clearance, never measured — and the map from the servo's **reported load** to
real newton-metres, which is the signal `smalldog_walker/contact.py` wants to use for foot
contact and which nobody has calibrated.

### Measurement → parameter

| # | stand | measurement | lands in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | stall torque at 12 V, per servo | `md.SERVO_STALL_NM` |
| 2 | A | angle vs applied torque → joint rate | new: the search's deflection model. Includes the printed fork and sleeve, which is the point — the gearbox alone is not what the foot hangs off |
| 3 | A | load-reversal loop → backlash + deadband | the search's angular resolution. Enumerating below it is enumerating noise |
| 4 | A + C | reported load counts vs real N·m | `smalldog_walker/contact.py`'s `contact_threshold` |
| 5 | A | hold torque vs case temperature vs time | the duty-cycle cost term. Nothing in this repo models it, and a search will happily pick a gait that cooks |
| 6 | B | no-load speed at 12 V | `md.SERVO_NOLOAD_RADS` |
| 7 | B | step response at ≥ 3 known J | `export_sim.MJ_KP`, `MJ_DAMPING`, `MJ_ARMATURE`, `MJ_FRICTIONLOSS` |
| 8 | B | goal-position tracking lag at 100 Hz | the enumeration's time step — the walker publishes at 100 Hz and assumes the joint is there |
| 9 | A + B | the torque–speed line between 1 and 6 | the feasibility filter itself |
| 10 | C | candidate trajectory → real foot path and force | end-to-end check of 1…9 |

1…9 are **per servo**, and they are not the same servo to servo. Run at least three and
quote a spread: the search's margin has to cover the worst one on the robot, not the mean.

**The servo's own telemetry is what is being calibrated.** The ST3215 reports position,
speed, load, voltage, current and temperature over its bus, and a motion search will run on
those reports — so the external instruments here (known masses, an independent encoder, a
load cell) exist to check what the servo says about itself, not to replace it.

## The stands

### A — torque bench (static)

`bench_base` + `torque_arm` + `protractor` + `enc_magnet_cap` + `enc_bridge`

The servo is clamped in `mini_dog.sleeve()` — the robot's own sleeve, full length, cable
window, thrust-clamp lug, both nut channels — and drives `mini_dog.fork()` on the two stock
aluminium hubs. What this stand measures is therefore **this robot's joint**, plastic and
all, and not a servo in a vice.

The load arm's outermost station is at

```
ARM_R = SERVO_STALL_NM / (2 kg · g) = 149.8 mm
```

which is derived, not styled: **one 2 kg mass at the outer station is exactly the catalogue
stall torque**, so full scale is a round number you can hang. Three stations, 50 / 100 / 150 mm:

| station | 1 kg | 2 kg | mass for full scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 mm | 0.490 N·m | 0.980 N·m | 6.00 kg |
| 100 mm | 0.980 N·m | 1.960 N·m | 3.00 kg |
| 150 mm | 1.470 N·m | 2.940 N·m | 2.00 kg |

The arm is one-sided, so its own weight is a torque about the joint at every angle. That is
a **tare**, not a defect: it is a rigid printed part, so `servo_bench.py` reports its mass,
the radius of its centre of mass and the moment that follows — 82 g at r = 66.0 mm →
**0.053 N·m at the horizontal**, 1.8 % of full scale — and the protocol subtracts
m·g·r·cos θ. A balanced beam would be better and cannot exist here: the counterweight would
have to swing through the buttress. **Weigh the printed arm before using it.** The 82 g is
solid volume × `md.PRINT_FILL_MEAN`, which is a mean over other parts.

Free sweep, scanned against the real solids: **110° up, 100° down**, both ends set by the
buttress. The protractor covers ±90° of that.

### B — inertia bench (dynamic)

`bench_base` + `inertia_disc` + `enc_magnet_cap` + `enc_bridge`

The same base; the arm comes off and a flywheel goes on. Its own inertia is computed from
the real solid through `export_sim.MP` — the same mass properties the URDF and the MJCF are
built from — and M8 bolts on the pocket circle step it up in weighed increments:

| bolts | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J (g·cm²) | 310 | 433 | 555 | 677 | 800 |

(3.1 × 10⁻⁴ … 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m², which brackets the shin about the knee.) Fill the pockets
**symmetrically only** — 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8, opposite pairs. An unbalanced rotor is not
dangerous at this scale, but it puts a once-per-rev disturbance into exactly the step
response the stand exists to measure.

The bare rotor needs 0.029 N·m to reach the catalogue no-load speed in 50 ms — 1 % of stall
— so the step response is torque-limited and readable rather than instantaneous.

### C — leg rig (end to end)

`leg_tower` + `foot_plate` + `cell_riser`

Not a second servo bench. It is the whole leg at the robot's own geometry, because what a
motion search gets wrong is never one joint — it is the knee carrying the shin's inertia
while the hip is somewhere the single-joint bench never put it. The cradle is
`mini_dog.roll_module()` itself, moved onto a flange, so the leg hangs off exactly the
structure it hangs off on the robot.

The foot lands on a plate carrying a 5 kg straight-bar load cell. That is the only force
anywhere in this repo that is **measured** rather than derived, and it is what closes
measurement #4. It is sized on `fea.py`'s own landing case — 36 N on one foot — so a 5 kg
(49 N) bar covers it with little margin; use a 10 kg bar if you intend to drop the leg.

Mount the flange to a board or a 2020 upright with the leg hanging past the edge. The roll
axis wants to be **187 mm** above the plate's top face, with the plate at x +24, y +76 from
the flange. Those come off the *posed* solid, not from `FOOT_Z`: the stance pose swings the
foot up and forward, and the nominal number puts the plate 13 mm low.

The four flange bolts go through from the board's far side with their nuts on the flange's
inboard face, where they stand ~8 mm proud into space the hip-roll fork sweeps. So their
centres have to clear r = 34 about the roll axis, and `servo_bench.py` measures that off the
real axis rather than trusting the coordinates — **+8.2 mm** as drawn. Nothing else would
catch it: the flange does not exist in the robot's own model, so `mini_dog.py`'s ROM scan
never sees it, and the leg would simply jam at one end of the roll travel and nowhere else.

Per-joint angle is stand A and B's job; on the leg rig you measure the foot.

### The stand must be held down

A printed stand loaded to full scale at 150 mm tips over. Its own weight restores
**0.67 N·m** against **1.73 N·m** demanded — so it is bolted to a board through six M4, or
it takes **1.8 kg** in the ballast tray for 2× margin. `servo_bench.py` computes both and
prints them every run; this is a requirement, not a caution.

## How the geometry is constrained

The one rule that shapes stands A and B is `mini_dog.py`'s own: **nothing may sit in
23 < r < 34 of a joint axis over the sleeve's length**, because the distal fork's spine
sweeps that annulus — and the fork here *is* `md.fork()`. On the robot the cradle escapes by
coming in from a sector the roll fork never reaches. This bench needs a large sweep in every
direction, so it uses the other exit: a plane at x = −(34.01 + 2) is tangent to the sweep
circle at the axis height and further from it everywhere else, so everything behind that
plane is clear at every angle. That plane is the buttress' front face, and it is why the
stand is an L and not a yoke.

**A yoke cannot exist.** The sleeve ends at |y| = 16.5 and the fork arms start at |y| = 17.9,
so there is nowhere for a second bearing wall to reach the sleeve from. The joint is
supported from one side, exactly as it is on the robot.

The buttress' front face runs straight down to the plate and does **not** rake forward under
the joint, which structurally it would like to: a rake out to the plate's front edge costs
the arm its downward sweep — the tip is at r = 156 and fouls such a face at −74°, against
the ±90° the protractor is drawn for. The footprint a rake would have bought comes from the
base plate instead.

Everything outboard of the fork's −y arm (|y| ≥ 28) is a plane nothing else can reach, so
the protractor and the flywheel both live there — which is why the two stands are never
assembled at once, and why the sweep check pairs the arm with the protractor and the rotor
with the bridge but never all four.

### Four checks that are failures, not warnings

`servo_bench.py` ends with them, in the same spirit as `mini_dog.py`'s `body clear:` line.
Each exists because something it now catches was actually wrong at some point in building
these:

- **`stand clear:`** — `bench_base`, `enc_bridge` and `protractor` are bolted into one rigid
  stand and nothing else notices when two of them occupy the same solid. It caught the
  sleeve collar standing 6 mm proud of the pad the bridge sits on.
- **`sweep`** — the free rotation of each moving part against the stand, on real solids. It
  runs against `bench_base(hollow=False)`, the stand's *silhouette*, and that is not a
  detail: a per-angle interference scan is static, so against the real hollow buttress the
  arm tunnels into its cavity and the scan reports a sweep that does not exist. It reported
  −110° that way, off a joint that stops at −105°.
- **`cell floats:`** — `foot_plate` and `cell_riser` must not share solid. A bending-beam
  load cell whose two ends are bridged reads a fraction of the force, or nothing, and looks
  exactly like one that works. Nothing downstream would ever tell you.
- **`flange bolts … clear`** — the leg tower's four bolt centres against the hip-roll fork's
  swept circle, measured off the real roll axis. The flange does not exist in the robot's
  own model, so `mini_dog.py`'s ROM scan cannot see it; a nut 3 mm too far inboard is a leg
  that jams at one end of the roll travel and nowhere else.

### The stand is not what you are measuring

Two things follow from that and are built in.

The encoder is referenced to the **buttress**, not to the table: the bridge grows off the
pad within ~40 mm of the sleeve, so whatever the stand does under load, the sensor and the
servo case do most of it together and the reading stays a joint angle rather than a stand
angle. What is left is small — `servo_bench.py` integrates Euler-Bernoulli over the
buttress' real section as it tapers and gets **0.033° at full scale**, against the degrees
the joint itself is expected to give. That integration is first order and is not a
substitute for `fea.py`: no shear, no stress concentration. It does not have to be exact; it
has to answer whether the stand is an order of magnitude stiffer than the joint, and it is
by two.

The protractor is the deliberate exception — it stands on the base plate, because at
r = 62 one degree is about a millimetre and it reads to a quarter of a degree at best. The
AS5600 is the instrument, at 0.088°/LSB. The protractor is the zero reference and the check
that the encoder has not lost a turn.

## Assembly

Stands A and B share everything up to step 4.

1. **`servo_gauge` first** (`mini_dog.py`'s, not a bench part) — a real ST3215 has to slide
   into the sleeve and the ⌀14 pattern has to line up.
2. Bolt `bench_base` to the board through six M4, **or** load the ballast tray with 1.8 kg.
   Do this before anything is on the arm.
3. Two M3 nuts into the thrust lug's channels → servo into the sleeve, connector down into
   the buttress cavity and the lead out the back → both thrust bolts in, evenly, until the
   case stops rocking. Nothing is added forward of the buttress' front face, so both bolts
   stay reachable from +x with the stand assembled — the same order as a leg.
4. Both aluminium hubs onto the servo. Then either:
   - **A:** `torque_arm` onto the hubs, M2.5 × 6 driven side, M2.5 × 7 passive side.
   - **B:** `inertia_disc` onto the hubs, same screws, through its axial bore.
   Both parts are bored ⌀26 on the axis so the passive-side screws can be reached at all —
   that bore is the only way a driver gets to those four heads.
5. Magnet into `enc_magnet_cap` (press fit, ⌀5.9 on a ⌀6.0 magnet; the fork arm backs it
   up, so nothing relies on glue) → cap onto the +y fork arm, swapping that arm's four
   M2.5 × 6 for **M2.5 × 8**. Check the magnet is the diametric one before it goes in.
6. Two M3 nuts into the pad's channels, which open on its +y face → `enc_bridge` on top,
   two M3 × 16 through its slots, snug but not tight. AS5600 into its pocket, two M2.
7. **Set the gap with the AGC register, not with a feeler gauge.** Power the sensor, slide
   the bridge along its slots until the AS5600's automatic gain sits mid-range, then
   tighten. Nominal is 2.5 mm magnet-to-IC, inside the 0.5–3 mm the part wants; the slot
   gives ±1.5 mm and the inboard end is a hard stop against the cap.
8. **A only:** two M3 nuts into the base plate's channels, which open on its −y edge →
   `protractor` down onto the plate, two M3 × 16. Zero it against the pointer with the arm
   horizontal and unloaded.

To swap servos, take off the bridge (2 × M3), the arm or rotor (8 × M2.5), and the thrust
bolts. The protractor and the nuts stay put.

**Stand C:** flange to a board or upright with 4 × M6, leg hanging past the edge → assemble
one complete leg per the robot's own order (`README.md`, "Assembly order", step 4) → cell's
fixed end down onto `cell_riser` with 2 × M5, riser to the board with 2 × M4 → `foot_plate`
onto the cell's free end with 2 × M4 through its spacer. Position it under the foot's
contact patch, 187 mm below the roll axis.

The cell only reads if its two ends are carried by two bodies that do not touch. The
platform floats on the free end alone; the slot in the plate's edge is there so you can see,
with the leg standing on it, that it has not settled onto the riser.

## What is not measured yet

Everything the stands exist to produce. Until they have been built and run:

- `SERVO_STALL_NM`, `SERVO_NOLOAD_RADS` and `SERVO_KG` stay vendor figures, and `SERVO_KG`
  stays marked **verify** in `mini_dog.py`.
- `MJ_*` stay the estimates `export_sim.py` flags them as.
- Joint compliance and backlash are not in the model at all — the ±0.9°/joint figure in the
  thrust-clamp note is arithmetic on the print clearance.
- `smalldog_walker/contact.py` has no calibration, which is why
  `/smalldog/foot_load` has no publisher on hardware.

Dimensions in `servo_bench.py` that are marked **verify** and are genuinely unconfirmed: the
AS5600 breakout outline and hole pitch (`ENC_PCB`, `ENC_PCB_HOLES`), the load-cell bar and
its bolt pattern and threads (`CELL_*`), and the 17 g nominal for an M8 × 20 and nut
(`BOLT_M8_G`) — weigh yours; the rotor's J table is only as good as it.

## This is not the planner

Nothing here generates or scores motions. `servo_bench.py` produces the CAD and the BOM for
the hardware that measures the constants such a planner needs, and the measurement →
parameter map above. Writing the search is a separate change, and it should not start before
there are numbers to feed it — a search over vendor figures is a search over a robot that
does not exist, which is the whole argument for building these.

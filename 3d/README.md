# Mini Robot Dog — V1 printed CAD

Parametric 12-DOF quadruped built around **12 × Feetech/Waveshare ST3215 (STS3215)** bus
servos. Everything structural is FDM-printed (PETG/ASA); no aluminium, no machining, no
external bearings — per `mini_dog_codex_handoff.md`.

The model is generated by [mini_dog.py](mini_dog.py). Re-run it after changing any parameter:

```bash
.venv/bin/python mini_dog.py     # writes out/step, out/stl, out/bom.json
.venv/bin/python render.py       # writes out/view_*.png
.venv/bin/python fea.py --all    # strength check - run after every change
.venv/bin/python export_sim.py --check   # writes out/sim/ (URDF + MJCF, flat + terrain)
```

Any change is re-checked for strength **and** re-exported to both simulations in the same
pass; the workflow and the current baseline are in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md).

## Servo interface — measured, not guessed

From the Waveshare ST3215 wiki downloads in [ref/](ref/), measured with [tools/](tools/):

| | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| case | 45.22 × 24.72 × 35.00 mm | `ST3215.step`, `ST3215.pdf` |
| rotation axis | along the **35 mm** dimension, 10.11 mm from the output-side end face, centred in the 24.72 width | drawing SCS215 |
| driven hub plate (25T side) | ⌀19.2, outer face **+20.30** from the case mid-plane | STEP |
| passive hub plate | ⌀19.2, outer face **−16.95** (recessed 0.55 into the case base, ⌀25 pocket) | STEP |
| hub-to-hub mounting span | **37.25 mm** | drawing |
| hub fixing | 4 × **tapped M3** on a **⌀14** bolt circle, at 0/90/180/270° | **hardware** — the STEP says ⌀2.5 and is wrong |
| hub plate thickness | driven **2.50** (z 17.80…20.30), passive **2.20** (−16.95…−14.75) | STEP |
| case side faces | **no threaded holes** — the case is gripped by form, not by screws | STEP |

That last row drove the whole architecture.

## Joint architecture

```
proximal link ──► SLEEVE : rectangular tube around the servo case, open on the axis.
                          Reaction torque goes into the 45×35 flats, not into screws.
                          Two M3 thrust screws through its back wall take up the play.
distal link   ──► FORK   : two arms lying on the two aluminium hubs, 4 × M3 each.
                          The arms straddle the sleeve, so the servo is also captured axially.
```

- the servo is **never** loaded through a printed thread or a single-shear horn;
- assembly = slide the servo into the sleeve, set the two thrust screws, bolt the fork to
  both hubs;
- the stock passive hub is used as supplied — no 628/685 bearings.

Range of motion is measured by swept boolean interference against the real solids
(`rom_scan()`), with the thrust screws, the hub screw heads, the cradles, the camera and
the GPS mast in the static set. It reads roll ±90, pitch ±90, knee ±110; ±90 is where the
scan window stops, not where the leg fouls.

### The thrust clamp

A form fit needs clearance to assemble, and clearance is backlash: on `CLR` alone the case
rocks ~±0.9° in its sleeve, ~±2.5° at the foot with three joints stacked. Two M3 jack
screws through the sleeve's back wall (−x end, away from the cable window) at y = ±5.5 push
the case forward onto the two legs the cable window leaves in the front wall. That **pins**
the case: turned either way it drives itself onto one thrust screw at the back and the
opposite front leg — play closed by geometry, not friction, which is why there are two.
Each threads into an M3 nut in a side-loaded channel in the lug.

Two rules, both measured:

- **Do not slit the sleeve into a C.** The −x end wall is the tube's only crossing of y = 0
  (the cable window ate the front one). A slit costs `thigh_A` 1.1 → 0.6 inter-layer SF at
  stall and 2.65 → 12.2 mm of deflection; tying the front wall back across the window
  recovers none of it.
- **Everything the clamp adds stays inside r < 23 mm of the joint axis**: the distal fork's
  spine sweeps 23…34 over the sleeve's length. The lug corner sits at 21.6 and is fine; a
  DIN 912 cap head on an M3 × 10 reaches **r = 24.2** and binds the joint over ±2…38° —
  the first assembled leg did exactly that. So the screw is **headless: an M3 × 10 set
  screw** (r = 20.96, 1.43 mm spare); × 12 still clears, × 14 does not. A button head
  reaches 22.98 and a countersunk head 23.08 — neither is a clearance. `thrust_clear()`
  prints the margin on every run; `thrust_bolts()` is in every `rom_scan`.

### The cradle joint

The hip-roll cradles are **not part of the tray**: `cradle_front` and `cradle_rear`, one off
each, each carrying both of that end's roll servos, four M3 × 12 per end. The tray went from
213 × 110 × 50 mm / 208 g to 126 × 92 × 50 / 127 g (cradles 38 g each), and a tray change
stopped meaning "reprint four cradles and unbolt four servos".

- **One part per end, not four quarters**: the two halves' rails overlap on the centreline
  (each reaches y = ∓14), so the quarters are not separable bodies.
- **Four screws and a register.** The screws see 22 N axial and 28 N shear at worst —
  nothing. What they do not replace is 795 mm² of welded face for **stiffness**: the roll
  axis is 27 mm outboard and the foot 187 mm below, so 0.1 mm of slop at the flange is
  0.7 mm at the ground. Each screw runs through a ⌀`CRADLE_REG_D` = 7 spigot, `CRADLE_REG`
  = 2 mm proud, into a pocket in a locally thickened tray wall. Spigots take shear and
  torsion; screws only clamp.
- **Where the screws may go is decided by the fork.** The flange is only `CRADLE_T` = 4 mm
  deep (its outer face is `FORK_GAP` behind the roll fork's rear arm) and an M3 nut does not
  fit in 4 mm, so nut bosses run out to `CRADLE_BOSS_X` = 71.5 (servo case at 72.5). That is
  legal because past the flange the only thing in the way is the arm's `ARM_R` disc — the
  spine's annulus is swept only outboard. Screws at (±17, ±10.5), nearest boss corner at
  r = 15.0 against `ARM_R` = 13.5. `rom_scan` checks the arm; `cradle_clear()` checks a
  driver reaches the heads; `cradle_head_clear()` checks the heads against the battery
  module (1.65 mm ISO 7380 heads on a seat 1.25 mm off the pack — hence `CRADLE_CB` = 2 mm
  of counterbore).
- **The flange is a frame**, and its opening is what lets the rear connector panel out.
- **`cradle_front` is the weakest printed part** — inter-layer SF 1.33 at `land3g`
  (`fea.py --orient`). The first FEA of it fixed a 1.4 mm notch and a 27.6 mm step in the
  strap (peak 53.7 → 29.7 MPa); the rest is structural, and the three obvious fixes each
  made it worse. `CLAUDE.md` has the rule.

### Reaching the fork screws

The fork's arms straddle the sleeve, so the fork goes on radially over a servo already in
its bore and its eight screws are always **last**, driven along the joint axis from outside
each arm. Whether a driver fits is a property of whatever sits behind that arm.

At the roll joint the inboard arm faces the chassis with `FORK_GAP` of air, and the fix was
the **assembly order**, not a hole: the cradle is a bolted part, so the fork goes on with
the cradle **in hand**. At that moment all four inboard screws are in open air — a ⌀5
driver is blocked by 0 mm³ against the cradle alone against 569 mm³ with the cradle on the
tray. One feature serves it: `FORK_DRIVER_R` = `HUB_BC`/2 + `DRIVER_D`/2 + `CLR` = 9.85, the
driver's swept circle relieved through the flange; without it the outermost screw fouls the
frame's rib by 38 mm³.

`fork_access()` is the check — a key-sized cylinder on each screw's real line, run until it
stands in open air — and it reads `all six arms open to a straight key within 40 mm`. Two
earlier answers (four coaxial ⌀6 bores from inside the tray, which reached one screw of four
while the probe passed because it stopped inside the bore; then two ⌀6 channels leaning 20°
through the tray's front corner, which worked and cost the chassis eight holes) are in git
history. The lesson: an access problem that keeps needing cleverer holes is a part boundary
in the wrong place. A chassis printed against the old geometry still has the holes and is
not wrong, just drilled.

**The screw heads are in the model.** The hip's inboard arm had 0.6 mm to the gusset and an
ISO 7380 head is 1.65 tall; `FORK_GAP` is 1.1, the passive arm carries a `FORK_CB` = 1.2
counterbore, `fork_screws()` sweeps all eight heads in every `rom_scan`, and `head_clear()`
prints the head's air and the tip's distance to the case.

| | mm |
|---|---|
| chassis tray | 126 × 92 × 50, walls 2.8, floor 3.0 |
| deck | 4 mm plate + 6 mm side lips; 137 × 92 print bbox with the Orange Pi tabs |
| wheelbase / track | 180 / 152 |
| hip-roll axis | X ±90, Y ±36, Z 0 (axis along X) |
| hip-pitch axis | X ±90, Y ±76, Z −30 (axis along Y) |
| thigh / shin | 75 / 82 |
| foot | ⌀26 TPU dome |
| joint width (over fork arms) | 46.2 |
| shin section, boss / shaft / ankle | 27.6×45.8 / 24×30 / 19.5×20.5 |

The hip-pitch axis sits **30 mm below** the hip-roll axis: that lifts the roll fork out of
the thigh's swing plane and buys ±80° of hip pitch instead of ±15°.

### Shin profile

The shin is a lofted, hollow, curved blade. Its shape comes from the **Waveshare DOG PRO
lower leg** in [ref/](ref/), measured by [tools/ref_ws_shin.py](tools/ref_ws_shin.py):

| | reference | ours |
|---|---|---|
| joint centres | 101.8 mm | 82 mm |
| thickness along the joint axis | **12.0 mm, constant** | 20 mm, near-constant |
| depth in the bending plane | 26.3 → 8.9 mm (×0.34) | 27.6 → 18.6 mm (×0.67) |
| centreline bow off the chord | 5.3 % of length | 5.4 % of length |

A blade: deep in the plane the knee bends it, thin along the axis, curved. Ours keeps the
proportions, not the sizes — PETG is ~40× less stiff than that part's aluminium.
[tools/ref_calf_profile.py](tools/ref_calf_profile.py) cross-checks the shape against
Unitree A1/Go1/Go2/B2, the MIT mini cheetah and Spot.

The profile is the `SHIN_PROFILE` table (u below the knee axis, fore-aft depth, lateral
thickness, bow, corner radius), interpolated with a monotone cubic and lofted. Things that
bite: the interpolation is monotone on purpose (an overshoot bulges the loft); the boss
section starts inside the fork spine so the union has no coincident faces; the shear-web
slab stops 0.5 mm short of the cavity's end caps because a tool crossing those spline faces
makes the OCC boolean silently return its input (`CLAUDE.md`); the cable-tie anchor goes
through the **lateral** wall, the neutral axis for the bending the knee does.

## Payload bays

- **Battery module** — 3S2P 6 × 21700 (Molicel P42A class) as a *module*: cells in two
  printed combs (`cell_holder` ×2), welded into a 3 × 2 brick, heatshrunk over cells and
  holder, with its BMS in a printed case (`battery_case` + `battery_lid`) that drops into
  the tray as one payload. 95.2 × 69.7 × 47.2 mm, z −22.8 … 24.4, **0.60 mm** of air to the
  deck (a foam strip). `CELL_D`/`CELL_L` are the P42A's datasheet maxima over the wrap.

  The combs exist because welding six loose cylinders into an aligned brick by hand was the
  step this design had no answer for: stand a cap on the bench, drop the cells in one at a
  time, push the second cap on; both terminal planes come out flush and open. A frame round
  the outside of the array does not fit (the module's side walls are 0.5 mm off the deck
  screws' nut bosses at |y| = 35.2), so the combs are **clipped flush with the outermost
  cells' tangent planes** in y and z and the module grows by the separator gaps alone
  (+0.8 z, +1.6 y). A 21.3 mm cell does not leave through the 8.4 mm flat an 0.8 mm collar
  leaves. `CELL_GAP` = 0.8 is the one number that costs height (0.6 → 0.80 mm deck gap); the
  printed web between bores is `CELL_GAP − CH_FIT` = 0.6.

  Two zones along x: the **BMS** (measured 60.13 × 37.2 × 5.9 mm) stands on edge against the
  rear wall — held by the wall and two ribs in x, two ribs in y, a ledge below, one bar under
  the lid above — so its leads and the pack's leave by one grommet slot without crossing the
  cells. The **brick** fills the rest to the thickened 7.5 mm front wall, the module's only
  screwed fixing: the lid's rear edge slides into a groove in the rear wall, its front edge
  takes two M2.5 into the front one. Two vents high in the rear wall — a cell that vents
  goes out the back, away from the deck. `BATT_X` = 5.0 is deliberately not re-centred; the
  brick sits 3.55 mm aft of centre, the direction the CoM wanted.

  What it cost: a case needs ~3.5 mm the bay did not have, and the only 3.5 mm near it was
  the IMU's slot, so the IMU moved onto the deck; the ESP32/URT-1 bay moved to x = −51; the
  eight deck screws all sit at |y| = 41 (at 38 the corner bosses bit 1.9 mm into the module).
- **ESP32 + Feetech URT-1 bay** at x = −51, behind the module. See `ref/urt1/README.md`:
  the URT-1 does not fit there and belongs on the bench; the robot wants a smaller
  UART-native adapter, ordered and awaiting measurement.
- **IMU** (BMI088 breakout, 20 × 15 mm): on the deck's **top** face on the centreline, on two
  2 mm standoffs inside the Orange Pi's own standoff gap, component face down, headerless
  (`ref/imu/README.md`). The position is `imu_xyz()` and both simulators emit their `imu`
  site there. `rl/checks/imu_placement.py` is what chose it: a board out on the deck beside
  the Pi reads 9.0 m/s² of ω × (ω × r) + α × r on the trot — 42° of apparent tilt — against
  25° on the centreline. The deck's cable window sits off-centre (`IMU_WINDOW`) to leave
  solid deck under the board; the two M2.5 form their own thread 5 mm into standoff and deck
  (*Screws into plastic*). Everything about the board is **verify**.
- **Rear connector panel**, laid out inside the cradle flange's frame opening (y ±43.11 by
  z ±9.36) and around its four bolt bosses: a 22 × 16 bus window, **XT30** (charge) at
  y = +32, the 3S JST-XH balance pass-through at y = −32, and the **XT60** (master
  disconnect / bench supply, on the fused P+) *above* the cradle at z = +20 — 16.5 mm of
  width does not fit inside the frame, and the band between `CRADLE_Z1` and `BODY_Z1` is
  9.64 mm for an 8.5 mm body. The two XT shells differ so a charger cannot be plugged into
  the servo bus. Each XT sits in a pocket in a locally thickened wall, goes in **from
  inside, before the deck**, and stands 1.5 mm proud on a 0.8 mm lip that takes the unplug
  force. Charge lead 18 AWG, bus lead 14 AWG for the 30–35 A fuse. `panel_clear()` probes
  every opening on every run.
- **Orange Pi 5 Pro**: four printed standoffs on the deck, 92 × 54 pattern — **verify**.
  That pattern puts the rear pair at x = −68 on a deck ending at −63, so the deck grows a
  tab under each rear standoff (`OPI_TAB_*`, guarded by `if OPI_TAB_X < -BODY_L/2`, so a
  measured pattern that lands on the deck deletes it). Moving `OPI_X` forward is not
  available: the Pi would hit the LiDAR pedestal. `OPI_BOX` = 100 × 62 × 20 over the deck
  is the envelope every exporter hangs `ELECTRONICS_KG` on *and* the keep-out `gps_mount`
  arches over — **verify** with the hole pattern.
- **LiDAR**: a **tilted seat**, not a mast. The Unitree L2 scans a hemisphere *above* its
  own base plane (NEGA mode buys 6° below it), so height buys no field of view. The seat
  leans **45° forward** (`LIDAR_TILT`), which lands the sensor's densest ring on the horizon
  and the lower rim on the ground **147 mm in front of the leading foot** in NEGA.
  - `LIDAR_SEAT_Z` is **derived**: no static bodywork may sit above the seat plane,
    `z + (x − LIDAR_X)·tan(tilt) < LIDAR_SEAT_Z`. The deck lip at (63, 35) binds it at 56.0;
    60.0 keeps 4 mm and leaves 31 mm under the seat for the RJ45. `mini_dog.py` prints
    `lidar clear:` or `!! LIDAR FOV` every run. Only static parts count — the legs sweep the
    cone every stride and get masked in software.
  - `LIDAR_X` = 42 is pinned: the base disc must clear the Orange Pi standoffs behind it
    (≥ 38.8) and the pedestal's deck bolts must land on a deck ending at 63 (≤ 44.1).
  - Two bolt circles: ours ⌀45 at 45° for the deck screws; the sensor's **⌀51 at
    22.5° + k·90°, 4 × M3 tapped 6 mm deep**, off the L2 drawing (⌀60 spigot, ⌀75 base).
    They cannot merge — ⌀51 for the deck needs the base disc at r = 30, which hits the Pi
    standoffs; the disc is ⌀52 (`LIDAR_BASE_R` = 26) for the same reason, and flat-cut in
    front at `LIDAR_BASE_FLAT` = 63.5 to clear the camera slot.
  - **No LiDAR guard, by decision.** A hemisphere referenced to the base plane cannot be
    caged — every bar above it is a permanent blind stripe, and the manual forbids even a
    glass plate. The low bow that used to sit under the cone protected little (the hip
    bracket reaches x = 114.3, level with the L2's nose) and cost 11 g at the very nose.
  - The pose lives once, in `mini_dog.lidar_pose()` / `lidar_com()`; both exporters read it.
- **Camera** — Weinan **WN-L2101.K203L** (Sony IMX415, 1/2.8", 8 MP, fixed-focus M12,
  USB 2.0 UVC), `camera_mount`. It puts a *name* on what the L2 puts a *shape* on.
  - **A 90 × 15 × 1.6 mm strip**, optical axis 70.59 mm from the connector end, ⌀14 M12
    holder 16.2 mm off the board — off the vendor drawing, transcribed in
    [ref/camera/](ref/camera/) with its two uncertain readings flagged.
  - **Exactly one place it fits, and all four walls are measured parts**: floor the cradle's
    top face at `CAM_LEDGE` = 15.36, ceiling the LiDAR base disc (z 29…35), back the chassis
    front face at x = 63, front the hip-roll fork's rear arm at `ROLL_X + FORK_Y0` = 68.1 —
    a disc sweeping r ≤ 34 over the roll ROM. The board lives in the 5 × 15 mm slot that
    leaves; only the lens goes past 68.1 (on the centreline it stays 36.3 mm from either
    roll axis). Landscape at z = 23 clashes with hip roll from +5°; under the belly the
    front legs sweep every height from +30° of pitch — both built and measured.
  - **`CAM_TILT` = 6°, a fit**: a tilted board eats 15·sin t of the 5 mm slot; 6° leaves
    ~1 mm front and back, 15° does not fit. The lens ends up 209 mm off the ground with an
    82 × 52° field: a 1.6 m face is in frame from 2.2 m out and ≥ 90 px across to ~4 m.
  - **The mount is a C-section on the gusset's own top face** — the only flat surface near
    the camera. The board slides in endwise and is trapped: a slot below, a wall in front
    of its upper half, a skirt behind, and a `CAM_CAP` = 1.2 mm roof over the near end that
    ties the front wall to the skirt. Both M3 × 20 live at the +y end, into nuts in slots in
    the gusset opening forward under the chin; the far end is keyed by a tongue. 4.3 g.
    ("Slides in endwise" is not a rigid straight slide — the ⌀14 lens holder cannot pass the
    front wall — so the real motion is a tilt-in.)
  - `camera_clear()` reports the highest elevation at which a solid shows in the frame; a
    face at 2.2…4 m sits at +19…+32°. Every static part reads **out of frame**, and
    `export_sim.py --check`'s render from the model's own `<camera>` agrees (0.0 % robot) —
    that render is also the only check that catches a wrong quaternion.
  - Two frames: `camera_link` is REP-103 (+X out of the lens), `camera_optical_frame` is
    REP-145 (+Z out, +X right, +Y down), differing by (−90°, 0, −90°). MuJoCo looks down its
    own −Z with +Y up, so [camera.py](camera.py) builds that quaternion from the axes.
  - **verify**: `CAM_OPT` (12 mm), `CAM_FOV_D` (the catalogue's single number, read as the
    diagonal), `CAM_RATE`, `CAMERA_KG` (12 g, unweighed).
- **GPS** — GY-NEO6MV2 + active patch, `gps_mount`. A patch antenna looks at the hemisphere
  above itself, and the only free sky on this robot is above the Pi and behind the L2 — free
  because the L2's cone leans 45° forward, so *up and behind* is below it (69 mm of margin,
  `lidar_fov_clear()` reads +48.1°). So: a trestle over the deck's **rear** pair of boss
  screws (M3 × 12 → M3 × 24), feet read from `DECK_SCREWS` rather than typed. The deck's
  stiffening lip gets a full-depth notch at that pair (`DECK_LIP_H`), because a ⌀9.6 pad at
  |y| = 41 would otherwise leave 0.05 mm of lip wall outboard of the hole.
  - **The kink in the arms is derived**: no run may lean more than 45° (print rule), and the
    arm must clear `OPI_BOX`'s top corner at (|y| = 31, z = 49). A straight 45° arm off the
    pad passes 8 mm too low, so the arm goes up first and kinks once at `GPS_KNEE` = 48;
    from there 45° buys as much inboard reach as height, so the platform's half-width and
    height are one number (arms landing at |y| = 24 → seat at 65). `gps_clear()` checks it.
  - The board is strapped by two ties at x = ±14, outside the 25 mm patch; its ends are
    held by nothing, because several board variants share the silkscreen.
  - The L2 blocks the forward sky below ~27°; nothing about the robot can fix that.
  - **Prints upside down** on the platform's top face (`tools/orient_scan.py`: 2018 mm² of
    bed against 126 the right way up, 339 mm² of overhang against 2238).
  - Optional: leave it off and put the two M3 × 12 back.

`_A` = FL + RR, `_B` = FR + RL (the two chiralities; front/rear are the same part rotated).
The cradles are two distinct prints, not a mirrored pair, and only `cradle_front` carries
the camera's nuts. Both print flange-down — the only build direction with the servo bores
vertical, which a press fit wants more than the stronger lying-flat orientation.

STLs in `out/stl/` are rotated into their print orientation (`PRINT_ORIENT`), which is
measured with `tools/orient_scan.py` and then sliced, not chosen by eye. For `hip_bracket`,
`thigh` and `shin` the bed-optimal direction is also the strongest (`fea.py --orient`); the
hip bracket on its Y face would save 17 g of support for a third of its interlayer margin.
Support mass is set by the parts' own internal ceilings, not the threshold angle.

Sliced in OrcaSlicer for a 0.4 nozzle at 0.2 mm (`out/gcode/summary.json`): body plate
~476 g / 14.6 h, legs 604 g / 21.2 h, feet 28 g / 2 h — **~1.1 kg and 38 h** for the
robot. The parts are thin-walled, so the walls set the mass: measured fill factors run
0.78–0.97 of solid (0.65 for the TPU feet), and that is `PRINT_FILL` in `mini_dog.py`
section 4. Re-slice and re-measure after any wall-count or line-width change. Splitting the
tray to print it flat was measured and does not pay.

### The bench fixtures — not part of the robot

`bench_rig.py` (a stand holding one ST3215 with its axis horizontal, two arms) and
`torque_rig.py` (a self-reacting C-frame with a kitchen scale in its throat) import the
sleeve, the thrust clamp and the hub pattern so the bench holds the servo the way the robot
does. Nothing there is in `PARTS` or the mass budget. `robot/README.md` has what to print
and how to use them.

| foam strip, ~1.4 × 20 × 90 mm (over the battery lid, under the deck — the gap is 0.60 mm) | 1 |
| M3 × 20 + M3 nut (camera mount → the chassis gusset) | 2 |
| M3 × 16 + M3 nut (LiDAR pedestal → deck, from underneath) | 4 |
| M3 × 12 (Unitree L2 → pedestal, into the L2's own M3 threads) | 4 |
| M3 × 24 (GPS mast + deck → tray boss, **replaces** the rear two deck screws) | 2 |
| cable tie, 2.5 mm (GPS receiver → platform) | 2 |

Lengths that are derived, and where a longer screw is *not* the safe direction:

- **thrust clamp: headless**, ceiling M3 × 12 (*The thrust clamp*).
- **hub screws, M3 × 6 button heads, both arms.** M3 because `HUB_BOLT_D` is measured on
  the hub. Past the hub a screw bottoms on the servo case, which the vendor FAQ says burns
  it. Driven: 4.0 arm + 2.5 hub + 0.3 to the case = 6.8, an M3 × 6 engages 2.0 of 2.5.
  Passive: 2.8 arm (after the `FORK_CB` = 1.2 counterbore) + 0.95 pedestal + 2.2 hub =
  5.95, with 0.5 of the case's 0.55 recess spare. `head_clear()` prints both.
- **Orange Pi standoffs, M2.5 × 8 not × 10**: the hole does not pass through the deck, so
  the screw has `OPI_STAND_H` + board = 8.6 mm and no more.
- **camera, M3 × 20**: head counterbore at 25.96, nut floor at 7.36, 18.6 mm to full
  engagement; × 16 does not enter the nut.
- **IMU, M2.5 × 8 thread-forming**: 1.6 of board then `IMU_TAP_L` = 5.0 of formed thread
  (2 D: the 2 mm standoff plus 3 mm into the deck's 4, not through). × 10 breaks out
  under the deck.
- **foot, M3 × 30**: head seats `FOOT_CB_Z` = 8 mm below `FOOT_Z` in the sole's ⌀6.4
  pocket, nut's far face 17.7 mm above `FOOT_Z`; nothing shorter is fully engaged.

**Nothing in a torque path threads into plastic.** Every structural screw not going into
the stock aluminium lands in a nut in a side-loaded `nut_slot()` whose walls hold the flats.
The slot opens toward the face still reachable at that point in the assembly order —
outward for the LiDAR legs, inboard in y for the corner deck bosses, along +x for the mid
pair, outboard for the Pi standoffs, forward under the chin for the camera, above the foot's
top face for the ankle bolt. No heat-set inserts. The corner deck channels are closed by the
battery module once it is in, so all eight deck nuts go in with the deck off and before the
module. No nuts at the hubs: both aluminium plates are tapped M3 and there is no room
(0.30 mm behind the driven hub, 0.55 behind the passive).

### Screws into plastic

Off the torque path a screw may thread straight into the print — a lid, a cover, a bracket
that holds nothing but itself. The test is whether a load path runs through it; if it does
or you are unsure, it gets a nut. Such a hole is `M3_TAP` = ⌀2.6 / `M25_TAP` = ⌀2.1 (radii in
`mini_dog.py`): just under the middle of the thread's minor-to-pitch band, minus ~0.1 mm for
an FDM hole printing undersize. ≥ 1 D of boss wall all round, 2 D engaged (a formed thread
in plastic strips at about one), and it is a **one-assembly** thread — anything that comes
off repeatedly wants a nut. Both radii are **verify**: print a coupon first.

## Assembly order

1. Print `servo_gauge`; check a real ST3215 slides into the sleeve and the ⌀14 pattern
   lines up.
2. Set servo IDs over the bus **before** assembly (URT-1 + Feetech tool;
   `robot/README.md` has the id scheme).
3. Per joint: two M3 nuts into the thrust lug's channels → servo into the sleeve → both
   thrust set screws in evenly until the case stops rocking → both aluminium hubs → fork
   arms to the hubs (the bottom arm's ⌀23 pad enters the case-base recess). Thrust screws
   first: once the fork is on its spine sweeps over the lug. **At the hips this happens
   with the cradle off the tray**: straight 2 mm key, through the flange's opening and the
   `FORK_DRIVER_R` relief.
4. Cradles onto the tray, **before the battery module**: four M3 nuts into each cradle's
   boss channels, spigots into their pockets, four M3 × 12 from *inside* the tray into the
   counterbored seats. The front seats sit 1.25 mm off the module's face, so heads first,
   pack after.
5. Legs: hip bracket → thigh → shin → press the TPU foot onto the ⌀18 spigot → M3 × 30 up
   through the foot into the nut in the ankle slot (the slot is above the foot's top face,
   so the foot stays removable).
6. Battery module, off the robot: one `cell_holder` on the bench, six cells in, second
   holder on → weld into a 3S2P brick → heatshrink over cells and holder → BMS down its
   rib slot, leads through the grommet → brick in → lid's rear edge into the groove, front
   edge down, 2 M2.5 into the front wall (a one-assembly thread — a pack opened often wants
   those holes drilled and nutted).
7. Chassis, in this order because half the nuts stop being reachable: 8 M3 nuts into the
   tray bosses' slots (**all eight before the module**) → XT60 and XT30 into their pockets
   from inside, balance lead through its pass-through → battery module into its seat, foam
   strip on its lid, servo bus out through the side ports (never across the top) → 4 M3
   nuts into the pedestal legs and the pedestal bolted to the deck **from underneath, deck
   off** → deck onto the tray → BMI088 onto its standoffs, 2 M2.5, **before the Pi** → 4
   M2.5 nuts into the standoff slots, Orange Pi, servo bus adapter → GPS mast onto the rear
   deck screws (M3 × 24), receiver into its rails and two ties (the Pi goes in first; the
   mast comes off to lift the Pi) → camera: 2 M3 nuts into the gusset slots under the
   chin, channel onto the ledge, tongue into its pocket, both M3 down, module in from the
   +y end → L2 onto the flange, 4 × M3 × 12 into its own tapped holes (6 mm deep; a longer
   screw bottoms in the blind hole and lifts the sensor off its seat).
8. Zero all joints at the mechanical zero pose (legs straight down) before powering the
   gait.

## Onshape import

`out/mini_dog_assembly.step` is the whole robot, one named instance per part plus servo
dummies, in robot coordinates; import as **Assembly**. `out/step/<part>.step` are the
individual parts. Onshape imports dumb solids — the history lives in `mini_dog.py`. Units
mm.

## Simulation export — ROS 2 and MuJoCo

[export_sim.py](export_sim.py) writes `out/sim/` from the same model. There is a **second**
exporter, `../ros2/smalldog_description/scripts/generate_model.py`, with a different link
decomposition (the foot is merged into the shin there) and its own collision primitives;
both import `mini_dog.py` and both must be re-run after a change (`CLAUDE.md` step 6).

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `mini_dog.urdf` | ROS 2 URDF. Meshes are `package://mini_dog_description/meshes/...`; `--mesh-uri relative` for non-ROS tools |
| `mini_dog.xml` | MuJoCo MJCF: flat floor, free base, 12 position actuators, IMU/LiDAR/GPS sites, a renderable `<camera>`, `stand` keyframe |
| `mini_dog_terrain.xml` | the same robot on the heightfield |
| `meshes/*.stl` | the printed solids in link-local frames, in mm (both files scale by 0.001) |
| `meshes/terrain.png` | the heightfield, 8 × 8 m, from [terrain.py](terrain.py) |
| `camera_view.png` | one frame from the model's own `<camera>`, by `--check` (needs Pillow) |

- SI out; frame +X forward, +Y left, +Z up, origin at the chassis centre in the hip-roll
  plane; zero pose = legs straight down.
- Joint axes are physical and identical on all four legs (`hip_roll` about +X, `hip_pitch`
  / `knee` about +Y); mirrored legs carry **mirrored limits**, not mirrored axes.
- Link masses and inertias are integrated from the real solids at measured print density
  (`PRINT_FILL`), plus a real ST3215 solid per joint, plus the payloads as boxes. The total
  cross-checks against `fea.robot_mass()` and the ROS 2 generator to the gram.
- Joint limits come from the ROM scan in `out/bom.json`; `--rom-step 2` re-scans.
- Visual = the meshes; collision = primitives (a mesh collider is slow and MuJoCo silently
  takes its convex hull).
- The GPS gets a `gps` site and a `gps_link` (+Z is the patch normal) and no simulated fix.

**Rough ground.** [terrain.py](terrain.py) generates the heightfield both exporters use:
seeded fractal noise, ±27 mm over a 160 mm wavelength, flat inside 160 mm of the origin so
the robot spawns level. It also beds an obstacle course (ramps, walls, logs; `COURSE`)
starting at x = 0.95 m — past the ~0.5 m a 5 s regression trot reaches, so that run still
measures the relief. `--no-terrain-obstacles` leaves it out; `--terrain-amp` /
`--terrain-wave` / `--terrain-seed` move it. A rotation there is a **quaternion**: both
MJCFs compile with `angle="radian"`, which reads a degree value as radians silently.

**The LiDAR, in simulation.** [lidar.py](lidar.py) writes a `lidar` site at the optical
centre (from `mini_dog.lidar_pose()`, so it leans with `LIDAR_TILT`) and the scan
parameters as `<custom><numeric name="lidar_*">` into the compiled model, because the other
consumer is C++ (`mujoco_ros2_control`) and cannot import Python: the numbers exist once, in
`mini_dog.py`'s LiDAR block, and both readers pick them out of the file they already load.
Cone half-angle 96° (`LIDAR_FOV_NEGA`); rate **62340 points/s → 5196 per frame at 12 Hz,
measured** (`ref/lidar/`); range 0.05…30 m and 20 mm 1σ noise **verify**. The scan pattern
is a Risley pair — non-repetitive, matching the L2's coverage and rate but **not** its
density profile (the real unit falls off monotonically from its axis; a rosette piles up at
the rim). Use the model for geometry, coverage and occlusion, not for how many returns an
object gets. No intensity. The same pattern exists in `mujoco_lidar.cpp`; change both.

## Verify before printing the whole set

1. `servo_gauge` fit — bore and ⌀14 circle. It cannot check the thread (the hub came back
   M3 after the gauge passed) or access; try a real screw, and read `fork access:`.
2. Orange Pi 5 Pro hole pattern (92 × 54) and `OPI_BOX` (100 × 62 × 20 — a keep-out, not
   just a mass box).
3. Unitree L2: bolt circle, envelope, RJ45 + DC barrel — measured. Still **verify**: the
   range window and range noise.
4. Rear-panel connector bodies — XT60 16.5 × 8.5, XT30 12.0 × 6.6, JST-XH 3S 13.0 × 6.0 —
   are catalogue. A pocket 0.3 mm small is a connector that does not go in.
5. Servo cable/connector envelope: the sleeve window is 15 × 12 mm at the far end face.
6. GY-NEO6MV2 board (36 × 26 × 1.6), its patch (25 × 25 × 8), 22 g for the pair — a
   bazaar part; measure the one in hand, especially the header and u.FL.
7. Camera: `CAM_OPT`, the FOV reading, the mass. BMI088: board thickness, the header.
8. `M3_TAP` / `M25_TAP`: print a coupon.

## Known limitations of V1

- Stance is tall (~160 mm ground clearance nominal): good for snow, less good for tipping.
- The thigh is a plain constant-section box with unblended corners.
- No shell/covers, no connector strain reliefs.
- The ROM scan is a 10° sweep; refine with `step=2` before committing to joint limits, and
  ±90° of roll/pitch is the scan window, not a measured stop.

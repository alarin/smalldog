# Mini Robot Dog — V1 printed CAD

Parametric 12-DOF quadruped built around **12 × Feetech/Waveshare ST3215 (STS3215)** bus
servos. Everything structural is FDM-printed (PETG/ASA); no aluminium, no machining, no
external bearings — per `mini_dog_codex_handoff.md`.

The model is generated by [mini_dog.py](mini_dog.py). Re-run it after changing any parameter:

```bash
.venv/bin/python mini_dog.py     # writes out/step, out/stl, out/bom.json
.venv/bin/python render.py       # writes out/view_*.png
.venv/bin/python fea.py --all    # strength check - run after every change
.venv/bin/python export_sim.py --check   # writes out/sim/ (URDF + MJCF)
```

Any change to the model is re-checked for strength **and** re-exported to simulation in the
same pass; see the workflow in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md).

## Servo interface — measured, not guessed

Downloaded from the Waveshare ST3215 wiki into [ref/](ref/) and measured directly with
the scripts in [tools/](tools/):

| | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| case | 45.22 × 24.72 × 35.00 mm | `ST3215.step`, `ST3215.pdf` |
| rotation axis | along the **35 mm** dimension, 10.11 mm from the output-side end face, centred in the 24.72 width | drawing SCS215 |
| driven hub plate (25T side) | ⌀19.2, outer face **+20.30** from the case mid-plane | STEP |
| passive hub plate | ⌀19.2, outer face **−16.95** (recessed 0.55 into the case base, ⌀25 pocket) | STEP |
| hub-to-hub mounting span | **37.25 mm** | drawing |
| hub fixing | 4 × ⌀2.5 clearance holes on a **⌀14** bolt circle, at 0/90/180/270° | STEP |
| case side faces | **no threaded holes** — the case is gripped by form, not by screws | STEP |

That last row drove the whole architecture.

## Joint architecture

```
proximal link ──► SLEEVE : rectangular tube around the servo case, open on the axis.
                          Reaction torque goes into the 45×35 flats, not into screws.
distal link   ──► FORK   : two arms lying on the two aluminium hubs, 4 × M2.5 each.
                          The arms straddle the sleeve, so the servo is also captured axially.
```

Consequences:
- the servo is **never** loaded through a printed thread or a single-shear horn;
- assembly = slide the servo into the sleeve, bolt the fork to both hubs;
- the stock passive hub is used exactly as supplied — no 628/685 bearings (per spec).

Range of motion is measured by swept boolean interference against the real solids
(`rom_scan()` in the script), not estimated.

## Geometry

| | mm |
|---|---|
| chassis tray | 126 × 92 × 50, walls 2.8, floor 3.0 |
| deck | 4 mm plate + 6 mm side lips |
| wheelbase / track | 180 / 152 |
| hip-roll axis | X ±90, Y ±36, Z 0 (axis along X) |
| hip-pitch axis | X ±90, Y ±76, Z −30 (axis along Y) |
| thigh / shin | 75 / 82 |
| foot | ⌀26 TPU dome |
| joint width (over fork arms) | 46.2 |
| shin section, boss / shaft / ankle | 27.6×45.8 / 24×30 / 19.5×20.5 |

The hip-pitch axis deliberately sits **30 mm below** the hip-roll axis: that lifts the
roll fork out of the thigh's swing plane and is what buys ±80° of hip pitch instead of ±15°.

### Shin profile

The shin is a lofted, hollow, curved blade, not a taper. Its shape comes from the
**Waveshare DOG PRO lower leg** that ships in [ref/](ref/) — the one real quadruped link
this repo owns — measured by [tools/ref_ws_shin.py](tools/ref_ws_shin.py):

| | reference | ours |
|---|---|---|
| joint centres | 101.8 mm | 82 mm |
| thickness along the joint axis | **12.0 mm, constant** | 20 mm, near-constant |
| depth in the bending plane | 26.3 → 8.9 mm (×0.34) | 27.6 → 18.6 mm (×0.67) |
| centreline bow off the chord | 5.3 % of length | 5.4 % of length |

So it is a **blade**: deep in the plane the knee bends it, thin along the joint axis, and
curved. Ours keeps the proportions, not the absolute sizes — PETG is roughly 40× less
stiff than the aluminium that part is milled from, so the blade stays much fatter.
[tools/ref_calf_profile.py](tools/ref_calf_profile.py) cross-checks the same shape against
Unitree A1/Go1/Go2/B2, the MIT mini cheetah and Spot.

The whole profile is the `SHIN_PROFILE` table in [mini_dog.py](mini_dog.py) — u below the
knee axis, fore-aft depth, lateral thickness, centreline bow, corner radius — interpolated
with a monotone cubic and lofted. Things that bite:

- the interpolation is **monotone** on purpose. A curve that overshoots its control points
  makes the loft spline bulge where nothing asked it to;
- the boss section sits 0.2 mm inside the fork on every face, and starts *inside* the fork
  spine, so the union has no coincident faces to trip over;
- the shear web is left standing by taking a slab out of the cavity, and that slab stops
  0.5 mm short of the cavity's end caps. A tool crossing those spline faces makes the OCC
  boolean silently return its own input — the part then comes out webless *and* with a
  shell that later cuts fail on just as quietly. `isValid()` does not catch it; check the
  section areas;
- the cable-tie anchor goes through the **lateral** wall, which is the neutral axis for the
  bending the knee actually does. On the fore-aft face it would sit on the extreme fibre
  and cost ~13 % of the section modulus.

## Payload bays

- 3S2P 6 × 21700 (Molicel P42A class): cradle for two layers of three, cells along X,
  ribbed separators, two strap slots. Sits on the floor, centred → low CG.
- 3S BMS: bay in the front, ESP32 + Feetech URT-1 bay in the rear.
- Orange Pi 5 Pro: four printed standoffs on the deck (92 × 54 pattern — **verify**).
- LiDAR: pedestal 44 mm above the deck, ⌀45 bolt circle, ⌀22 cable core, near the body
  centre (**verify against the Unitree L2 base**).

nothing threads into plastic.

## Assembly order

1. Print `servo_gauge`, check a real ST3215 slides into the sleeve and the ⌀14 pattern lines up.
2. Set servo IDs 1…12 over the bus **before** assembly (URT-1 + Feetech tool).
3. Per joint: slide the servo into the sleeve → fit both aluminium hubs to the servo →
   bolt the fork arms to the hubs (bottom arm's ⌀23 pad enters the case-base recess).
4. Legs: hip bracket → thigh → shin → press the TPU foot onto the ⌀18 spigot, M3 through.
5. Chassis: battery + BMS in the tray, route the servo bus out through the side ports,
   deck on top, Orange Pi + ESP32/URT-1, LiDAR pedestal last.
6. Zero all joints at the mechanical zero pose (legs straight down) before powering the gait.

## Onshape import

- `out/mini_dog_assembly.step` — the whole robot, one named instance per part plus servo
  dummies, already in robot coordinates. Import as **Assembly**; every solid keeps its name.
- `out/step/<part>.step` — individual parts if you want to rebuild features on top of them.
- Onshape imports STEP as dumb solids (no feature history). That is expected here: the
  history lives in `mini_dog.py`. Change a parameter there and re-import, or use the
  imported solid as a base and add Onshape features on top.
- Units are mm; no scaling needed.

## Simulation export - ROS 2 and MuJoCo

[export_sim.py](export_sim.py) writes `out/sim/` from the same parametric model:

> There is a **second** exporter of this CAD: `../ros2/smalldog_description/scripts/
> generate_model.py` builds the ROS 2 package's own description (different link
> decomposition, its own collision primitives) by importing the same `mini_dog.py`.
> Change the model and both must be re-run — see step 6 of *Verifying a change* in
> [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md). All masses and densities come from `mini_dog.py` section 4;
> no downstream file keeps a copy.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `mini_dog.urdf` | ROS 2 URDF. Meshes are `package://mini_dog_description/meshes/...`; pass `--mesh-uri relative` for non-ROS tools. |
| `mini_dog.xml` | MuJoCo MJCF: floor, free-floating base, 12 position actuators, IMU site, `stand` keyframe. |
| `meshes/*.stl` | the printed solids, in link-local frames, still in mm (both files scale by 0.001). |

- SI out: metres, kilograms, radians. Frame is mini_dog's - +X forward, +Y left, +Z up,
  origin at the chassis centre in the hip-roll plane. Zero pose = legs straight down.
- Joint axes are physical and identical on all four legs: `hip_roll` about +X,
  `hip_pitch` / `knee` about +Y. The mirrored legs carry **mirrored limits**, not mirrored
  axes, so a positive `hip_pitch` swings every leg the same way in world terms.
- Link masses and inertias are integrated from the real solids at the BOM's effective
  density (feet in TPU, everything else PETG), plus a real ST3215 solid at its catalogue
  mass on each joint, plus the battery, the electronics and the LiDAR as boxes. Total
  **2.098 kg**, cross-checked against `fea.robot_mass()` (2.103 kg — the difference is
  `servo_gauge`, a test print that is not part of the robot) and against the ROS 2
  description generator, which reports the same 2.098 kg.
- Joint limits come from the ROM scan in `out/bom.json` (coarse 10 deg); `--rom-step 2`
  re-scans them. `hip_roll` currently reports +-90 deg, which is the scan's own window
  rather than an interference limit - the export flags that instead of hiding it.
- Visual = the printed meshes; collision = primitives (chassis box, leg capsules, foot
  spheres), because a mesh collider of a lightened bracket is slow and, in MuJoCo, silently
  replaced by its convex hull.
- **Estimates, not measurements:** the MuJoCo joint damping / armature / friction-loss and
  the position-servo gain. The ST3215 gearbox is a black box; tune them against hardware.

`--check` loads both files in MuJoCo, drops the robot into its stand pose and reports base
height, feet on the ground and whether the two files agree on mass.

## Verify before printing the whole set

1. `servo_gauge` fit — the one thing that can invalidate everything else.
2. Orange Pi 5 Pro hole pattern (currently 92 × 54).
3. Unitree L2 base bolt circle (currently ⌀45) and cable exit.
4. BMS outline (currently 64 × 27 × 13).
5. Servo cable/connector envelope: the sleeve window is 15 × 12 mm at the far end face.

## Known limitations of V1

- Stance is tall (~160 mm ground clearance at the nominal pose). Good for snow, less good
  for tipping — crouch harder in the gait, or shorten `L_THIGH` / `L_SHIN`.
- The thigh is still a plain constant-section box between its two joints. That matches
  what the reference legs do there, but its corners are unblended, unlike the shin's.
- No IMU mount, no shell/covers, no connector strain reliefs yet.
- The ROM scan is a 10° sweep; refine with `step=2` before committing to joint limits.
- The Waveshare `ROBOTIC DOG.step` supplies the **shape** of the shin and nothing else.
  Its joint spacing does not transfer — it is an aluminium-plate design with a single-shear
  servo horn, and this robot is a printed sleeve-and-fork one.
